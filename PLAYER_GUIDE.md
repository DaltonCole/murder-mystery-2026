# Welcome to the Masquerade

Tonight, high society is hiding something.

Somewhere in this room, behind the powdered wigs and candlelight and outrageous good manners, a revolution is being plotted. And somewhere behind *that*, something even the revolutionaries don't know about is quietly pulling strings. You're about to find out which of these worlds you belong to — and by night's end, so is everyone else.

This is Murder Mystery 2026: a live, phone-in-hand social deduction game for one glittering Regency evening. Think Bridgerton, but everyone at the ball is also, secretly, a spy.

## The mask and the app

Every guest tonight gets a mask you'll actually wear — and a second, invisible one on your phone: your character and your secret allegiance, viewable only by pressing and holding on your screen. Nobody else gets to see it. Not your best friend, not your date, not the person you've been quietly suspicious of all evening. Only Dalton, your host, is ever allowed to see your screen. Guard it the way you'd guard your actual face at a masquerade.

Check your role the moment the game starts, and carry it quietly the rest of the night. Some information is worth sharing strategically. Some — you'll see below — should never be shared at all.

## Three factions, one ballroom

You'll be sorted into one of three sides. Two of them get named out loud at the start of the night; the third, you'll have to discover for yourself.

**The Ton** are high society itself — the aristocrats, the establishment, the people whose portraits hang on the walls. They don't know it yet, but a revolutionary is hiding in their midst, and their whole night is a slow hunt to unmask that person before it ends.

**The Uprising** is that revolutionary's movement — a resistance trying to make it to sunrise with its leadership intact. Here's the twist: even the Uprising's own rank and file don't know who their leader is at the start. That's not a bug, it's how real underground movements survive — nobody can betray a secret they were never told. If you're Uprising, your job is to hold the line, protect your side's numbers, and trust that leadership is out there somewhere in the crowd.

**The Cult** is the side nobody names at the top of the night. It's small, it's patient, and it's playing the other two against each other for its own private ends. If this is you, you start the night with a real edge: everyone around you is busy fighting a war they think only has two sides.

The Ton has the numbers. The Uprising has the underdog's advantage. The Cult has the secret. Nobody — including you, depending on who you turn out to be — will know for certain how the night actually ends until it's revealed.

(Arrive fashionably late and you'll join instead as a Servant — your own separate track, with your own tasks and your own leaderboard, watching the whole drama unfold up close without being pulled into the three-way fight yourself.)

## The shape of the night

The evening plays out over several rounds, a lighthearted intermission, and one bigger finale at the end. Every round blends a bit of everything:

- **Tasks and mingling.** Small prompts land on your phone that nudge you toward people you might not otherwise talk to. Go meet people, knock out a few light tasks, and pay attention to what you notice along the way.
- **Contest games.** A couple of rounds turn into honest-to-goodness party games, played in teams, testing Strength, Creativity, and Intelligence. They're fun in their own right — but how your side performs quietly ripples into the larger story in ways you're not told about in the moment.
- **The Denouncement.** Then, every single round, the room turns serious for a few minutes. Someone gets accused. The room argues it out. Everyone casts a secret ballot. And someone — occasionally more than one someone — gets Cast Out.

The Denouncement is the heartbeat of the night. Nominations happen privately through the app first, so the room only ever debates the couple of people who actually got named by their peers — no open-floor free-for-alls. The debate itself is loud, fast, and entirely up to you; nobody moderates who speaks. Then everyone votes in secret, the tally goes up on screen, and Dalton reads out who's been Cast Out.

You will almost never be told *why*, or which faction they belonged to. That mystery is the whole point — Lady Whistledown's Society Papers publishes deliciously unhelpful gossip about it after every round, and that's genuinely all the room has to go on. Hold your certainty until the very last Denouncement of the night, when — and only when — everything comes out at once.

## If you get Cast Out

Take a breath: this is not the end of your night. Getting Cast Out means your mask comes off and you're escorted over to sit with the Servants — but you keep playing. You'll keep doing tasks, keep playing the contest games, and your results still count for your original side, exactly as if you'd never left the fight. You simply can't vote or use your abilities anymore, and you get the small consolation of seeing your own full character revealed on your phone.

You'll also get pulled into some genuinely fun jobs behind the scenes — helping score a contest zone, or joining "the Gallery" to place a prediction on how the whole thing ends. Getting Cast Out early is, unofficially, one of the better seats in the house.

## Before you go in

A few practical things to know walking in:

- **Bring your phone, charged.** Your role, your tasks, and every ballot you cast all live there.
- **Never show your screen to anyone but Dalton.** The entire game depends on that one rule.
- **If you turn out to be the Uprising's leader, tell absolutely no one — not even your own side, ever.** You'll understand exactly why once you're in it.
- Beyond that: talk to everyone, ask the question you're not sure you should be asking, and don't take anything you hear at face value. Everyone in this room is wearing at least one mask.

The candles are lit, the wine is poured, and somebody here is not who they say they are. Enjoy the ball.
